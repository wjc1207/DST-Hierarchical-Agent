Don't Starve Together strategic reference

This is background knowledge, not a recipe catalog or live observation.
Use query_recipes for actual ingredients, quantities, output, technology,
unlock/prototype conditions, supported status and current craftability. This
includes familiar recipes: character, skills, settings and mods can change them.
A stale query row or an incomplete recipe index is not proof of craftability
or of a recipe's absence. Do not fill missing facts with remembered recipe costs.
Use query_places for known buildings and home. The control contract and planning
instructions define supported actions, task semantics and execution restrictions.
General game knowledge cannot make an unavailable action executable.

Survival and reserves:
- Prepare usable light before darkness, and food before hunger becomes urgent.
  Consider health and nearby threats before optional travel or technology goals.
- Secure the minimum ingredients for an immediate survival need before gathering
  larger reserves. Do not make urgent light wait for campfire materials and spares.
  Query the chosen recipe to determine that minimum; do not assume fixed costs.
- Reserve targets need a concrete expected shortage. Consider current stock,
  consumption, intended travel and uncertainty. Completing a task or reaching a
  new planning window is not itself a reason to increase stock targets.
- A hunger goal is about observed hunger, not simply picking up one food item.
  Food on the ground is not carried food. Use currently supported safe-food
  actions; do not assume every edible-looking item is supported or safe.

Light:
- Day, dusk and night are distinct phases. Dusk alone is not a reason to stop
  safe work or defer all remaining goals to tomorrow. Weigh the current route,
  threats and usable light before choosing nearby work, return or residence.
- Portable light can accompany travel; stationary fires do not illuminate the
  entire route away from them. A fire structure's presence does not prove it is
  burning, and a nearby lit fire does not guarantee the whole path is illuminated.
- Judge a torch by remaining fuel in [0,1], not item count alone. Missing fuel or
  fire lifetime is unknown. Account for both carried and equipped items without
  counting one item twice. Other lighting items may have different use conditions.
- Fuel consumption and night length can vary with weather, seasons, settings and
  modifiers. Phase/progress alone does not establish exact remaining night time
  or effective lighting duration. Respect the runtime's current lighting rules.

Resources and tools:
- A recipe being craftable does not mean its tool is already carried. Work needs
  a usable tool with sufficient remaining durability for the target.
- Avoid circular prerequisites: a boulder that drops flint is not an obtainable
  source of first-tool flint without a usable pickaxe. Seek loose flint or another
  currently obtainable source instead. Loose drops do not require mining tools.
- Standard rock1 is not a gold-bearing source; rock2 is. Verify the observed target
  and available work actions rather than treating every rock as interchangeable.
- Trees and rocks can yield secondary materials. Check the observed inventory
  and remaining drops after work before starting a separate search. Tree size,
  species, season and burnt state may affect work or yield; use observed facts.

Crafting and technology:
- Plan material and tool prerequisites explicitly from queried conditions.
  Craftability does not create demand: make items that advance an actual need.
- Science Machine and other station visits provide access only when the current
  recipe conditions permit it. A completed visit does not prove the character is
  still in technology range or that a recipe was permanently unlocked.
- A buffered structure may already have paid its material cost. Query its state
  before requesting another batch of ingredients. Placement still needs a valid,
  reachable site clear of relevant hazards and combustible surroundings.
- Materials consumed or a build request sent are not proof of completion. Use
  observed outputs, constructed entities and execution results.

Places and exploration:
- Buildings are remembered instances; use their queried IDs rather than invented
  coordinates. A home is an explicit location, not a promise of light or supplies.
  A building outside view is unconfirmed, not necessarily destroyed.
- Regions use topology identity, not ground-tile identity. A region may contain
  mixed terrain, and different regions may share a biome. Observed next-exit
  distance is not necessarily the full distance to a destination.
- Klei room resource priors describe possible initial spawns in known room types,
  not guaranteed remaining stock or probabilities. Layouts and settings can change
  generation. Actual obtainable sources take precedence over terrain guesses.
- Historical resources_seen entries do not prove a source is still obtainable.
  An entrance observation, blocked path or temporary hazard is not a full survey
  and does not prove a resource is absent. Prefer fresh source evidence.
- Similar desert terrain does not establish an oasis. Avoid inferring unobserved
  resources or safety from ground appearance alone.
- A potential threat may attack on approach even when it is not actively chasing.
  Consider the approach and return route, not only the destination. Recently
  observed enemy locations may remain dangerous after fleeing.
